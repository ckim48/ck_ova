Joint-power AO diagnostic v3

Motivation. The original solver optimizes analog amplitudes with the previous
digital powers fixed, then replaces digital powers by the componentwise SIC
minimum. At a subsequent iteration, tight digital decoding constraints can
restrict the analog interference level. Optimizing both amplitudes together
at a fixed receive vector permits coordinated movement of that level and the
required digital powers. This is a numerical improvement within the original
physical problem, not a relaxation of its peak, battery, decoding or error
constraints.

Change. wireless_joint_v3.py is an isolated copy of immutable wireless.py.
Inside improve(), conic_step(receiver, digital) becomes conic_step(receiver).
The latter joint SOCP was already used for the original initialization. The
same receive weighted-median update, initial receiver grid and iteration cap
are retained. The minimum SIC powers are restored after each joint solve.
An independent test checks SINR, energy accounting, peak/battery constraints,
RMS envelope and nonincreasing energy, including nonzero residual SIC.

Fairness. Apply the joint update to Hybrid, phase-separated transmission and
mixed AirComp. Preserve original feasible incumbents for every setting and
select the lower energy; joint paths need not beat every old local solution.
Same legacy-round updates/channels, total symbol budget, peak power, overhead,
quantizers and error budget within each setting. Phase split uses all seven
original grid fractions; its digital phase continues to use NOMA. Full TDMA
and globally optimal time allocation are not claimed.

Grid fixed before solving: bits 8,6; relative RMS tolerance 0.2,0.6; phase
fractions 0.125,0.25,0.375,0.5,0.625,0.75,0.875. The two tolerances represent
the original protocol and an earlier CPU regime candidate, not test-set
selection. All cases, failed searches, old and new energies remain visible.

Interpretation. One CPU-reconstructed round is a solver diagnostic only.
The conservative envelope and conditional Gaussian expected error do not
establish retained utility or unlearning. A later full sequential training
run and final retained-retraining reference are required for those claims.
The original training code, manuscript, results and running GPU queue remain
unchanged. No assertion of global convergence or global optimality is made.
